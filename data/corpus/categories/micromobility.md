# Micromobility: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: e-scooters, e-bikes, bike sharing, scooters. Sector: Mobility & Travel.*

## Executive summary

Micromobility reached 28% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €16 per month. The category is growing solidly (+8.4% spend vs the 2025 wave). Shared e-bikes are replacing short ride-hail and transit trips; regulation (e-scooter bans) reshapes the market city by city.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 28%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €16
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €52
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +8.4%
- Buyers in panel: 1,670 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards men: 36% of men bought versus 20% of women. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 25% among 16-19 year-olds to 30% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Spain (31%) and lowest in the UK (23%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€20 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 33%, compared with 24% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by Friends & family (29% of buyers), followed by In store (24%) and TikTok (19%). TikTok accounts for 24% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 17% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (14% vs 12%).

The main purchase channel is Brand website / app (60%), then Subscription (21%) and Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (10%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is convenience (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (29%) and sustainability (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 66% of buyers, 5 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are safety and regulation and bans.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

41% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (16 points above the panel average of 25%). 3.8% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (13 points below the panel average of 17%), and 31% usually repurchase the same brand (7 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **E-bike passes**
- **Scooter bans**
- **15-minute cities**

## Implications for marketers

Offer monthly passes and lead on safety; partner with universities and employers.

- Prioritise Friends & family for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 29% of buyers.
- Build the offer around convenience, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Brand website / app, which carries 60% of purchases.
