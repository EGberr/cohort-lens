# Ride-hailing: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: Uber, taxis, ride sharing, rides. Sector: Cars & Mobility.*

## Executive summary

Over the past year, 53% of the Gen Z panel purchased ride-hailing, spending an average of €20 per month. The category is growing modestly (+2.7% spend vs the 2025 wave). Late-night safety, not convenience alone, drives Gen Z ride-hailing — especially for young women — while surge pricing is the main source of churn.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 53%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €20
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €127
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +2.7%
- Buyers in panel: 3,165 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 56% of women bought versus 49% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 47% among 16-19 year-olds to 59% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Germany (59%) and lowest in Italy (41%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€25 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 61%, compared with 49% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by Friends & family (36% of buyers), followed by Search engines (19%) and In store (14%). TikTok accounts for 14% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 12% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (21% vs 16%).

The main purchase channel is Brand website / app (82%), then Subscription (18%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is convenience (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (30%) and quality & durability (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 71% of buyers, 10 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are surge pricing and safety concerns.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

21% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (5 points below the panel average of 26%). 2.3% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (15 points below the panel average of 17%), and 39% usually repurchase the same brand (in line with the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among ride-hailing buyers, the most common product type is Late-night ride (52% of buyers). Late-night ride over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (29% of its buyers), while Airport transfer skews older (45% aged 25-29).

- **Late-night ride**: 52% of category buyers, typical price €18, interest stable, mostly bought via Brand website / app.
- **Airport transfer**: 26% of category buyers, typical price €45, interest stable, mostly bought via Brand website / app.
- **Ride subscription pass**: 23% of category buyers, typical price €10/month, interest rising, mostly bought via Brand website / app.

## Trends to watch

- **Safety features**
- **Price-predictable passes**
- **Car-free lifestyles**

## Implications for marketers

Feature safety tools prominently and offer predictable pricing passes for night-time trips.

- Prioritise Friends & family for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 36% of buyers.
- Build the offer around convenience, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Brand website / app, which carries 82% of purchases.
