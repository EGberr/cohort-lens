# Ride-hailing: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: Uber, taxis, ride sharing, rides. Sector: Mobility & Travel.*

## Executive summary

Over the past year, 53% of the Gen Z panel purchased ride-hailing, spending an average of €20 per month. The category is growing modestly (+2.7% spend vs the 2025 wave). Late-night safety, not convenience alone, drives Gen Z ride-hailing — especially for young women — while surge pricing is the main source of churn.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 53%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €20
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €128
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +2.7%
- Buyers in panel: 3,162 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 55% of women bought versus 50% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 46% among 16-19 year-olds to 58% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Germany (56%) and lowest in the UK (50%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€25 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 62%, compared with 48% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by Friends & family (35% of buyers), followed by Search engines (20%) and TikTok (14%). TikTok accounts for 15% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 13% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (22% vs 20%).

The main purchase channel is Brand website / app (82%), then Subscription (18%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is convenience (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (30%) and quality & durability (18%). Price is the decisive factor for 70% of buyers, 9 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are surge pricing and safety concerns.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

21% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (4 points below the panel average of 25%). 2.6% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (15 points below the panel average of 17%), and 40% usually repurchase the same brand (in line with the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Safety features**
- **Price-predictable passes**
- **Car-free lifestyles**

## Implications for marketers

Feature safety tools prominently and offer predictable pricing passes for night-time trips.

- Prioritise Friends & family for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 35% of buyers.
- Build the offer around convenience, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Brand website / app, which carries 82% of purchases.
