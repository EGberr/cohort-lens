# Specialty Coffee: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: coffee, iced coffee, matcha, coffee shops. Sector: Food & Beverage.*

## Executive summary

65% of Gen Z consumers bought specialty coffee in the last twelve months, spending an average of €33 per month. The category is growing solidly (+7.5% spend vs the 2025 wave). Coffee shops function as Gen Z's 'third place' and status symbol; iced and matcha drinks out-sell hot coffee in this cohort, and the cup is part of the content.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 65%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €33
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €260
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +7.5%
- Buyers in panel: 3,911 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 71% of women bought versus 60% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 58% among 16-19 year-olds to 70% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in the US (70%) and lowest in France (59%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€40 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 74%, compared with 60% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (30% of buyers), followed by Friends & family (23%) and Instagram (22%). TikTok accounts for 34% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 27% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (6.9% vs 5.3%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (65%), then Brand website / app (13%) and Subscription (9.5%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is aesthetics & self-expression (42% of buyers cite it first), ahead of quality & durability (29%) and community & belonging (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 58% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are cost of daily purchases and sugar in specialty drinks.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

38% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (12 points above the panel average of 25%). 8.0% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (9 points below the panel average of 17%), and 46% usually repurchase the same brand (7 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Design for the photo (cups, colours, seasonal menus) and use loyalty apps to convert a daily habit into a programme.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 30% of buyers.
- Build the offer around aesthetics & self-expression, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 65% of purchases.
