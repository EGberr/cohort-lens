# Skincare: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: skin care, serums, moisturiser, SPF. Sector: Beauty & Personal Care.*

## Executive summary

Over the past year, 78% of the Gen Z panel purchased skincare, spending an average of €36 per month. The category is growing solidly (+9.5% spend vs the 2025 wave). Gen Z skincare buyers behave like ingredient analysts: 'niacinamide' and 'ceramides' outrank brand names in search, and routines are assembled from several brands rather than one hero line.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 78%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €36
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €334
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +9.5%
- Buyers in panel: 4,689 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 85% of women bought versus 70% of men. Penetration is similar across age bands (16-19: 77%, 25-29: 79%). By market, penetration is highest in the US (81%) and lowest in the UK (76%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€43 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 84%, compared with 75% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (44% of buyers), followed by Instagram (18%) and Friends & family (12%). TikTok accounts for 49% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 40% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (11% vs 7.4%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (29%), then TikTok Shop (24%) and Online marketplaces (18%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 26% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 23% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is creator recommendation (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of quality & durability (29%) and health benefits (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 57% of buyers, 4 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are ingredient confusion and price of premium actives.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

48% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (22 points above the panel average of 26%). 42% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (25 points above the panel average of 17%), and 39% usually repurchase the same brand (in line with the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among skincare buyers, the most common product type is SPF 50 daily fluid (37% of buyers). SPF 50 daily fluid over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (30% of its buyers), while Vitamin C serum skews older (38% aged 25-29).

- **SPF 50 daily fluid**: 37% of category buyers, typical price €18, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Vitamin C serum**: 34% of category buyers, typical price €24, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Ceramide barrier cream**: 29% of category buyers, typical price €16, interest stable, mostly bought via Physical store.

## Trends to watch

## Implications for marketers

Lead with ingredient transparency and dermatologist-backed proof points; sell routines as modular bundles rather than single-brand regimens.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 44% of buyers.
- Build the offer around creator recommendation, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 29% of purchases.
