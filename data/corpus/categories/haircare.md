# Haircare: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Zentrend. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: shampoo, hair products, hair oil, conditioner. Sector: Beauty & Personal Care.*

## Executive summary

72% of Gen Z consumers bought haircare in the last twelve months, spending an average of €19 per month. The category is growing modestly (+6.2% spend vs the 2025 wave). The 'skinification' of hair is real: scalp serums and bond-repair treatments are the growth engines, while basic shampoo remains a price-led supermarket purchase.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 72%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €19
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €162
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +6.2%
- Buyers in panel: 4,291 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 78% of women bought versus 64% of men. Penetration is similar across age bands (16-19: 70%, 25-29: 73%). By market, penetration is highest in Spain (78%) and lowest in Italy (66%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€23 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 75%, compared with 70% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (35% of buyers), followed by In store (18%) and YouTube (16%). TikTok accounts for 38% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 32% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (8.6% vs 7.3%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (42%), then Online marketplaces (23%) and TikTok Shop (15%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 16% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 15% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is quality & durability (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of creator recommendation (30%) and price / value for money (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 61% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are unclear results and products not suited to hair type.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

37% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (11 points above the panel average of 26%). 33% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (16 points above the panel average of 17%), and 43% usually repurchase the same brand (5 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among haircare buyers, the most common product type is Everyday shampoo (50% of buyers). Everyday shampoo over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (29% of its buyers), while Scalp serum skews older (39% aged 25-29).

- **Everyday shampoo**: 50% of category buyers, typical price €6, interest stable, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Bond repair treatment**: 28% of category buyers, typical price €28, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Scalp serum**: 23% of category buyers, typical price €22, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.

## Trends to watch

- **Scalp care**
- **Bond repair**
- **Curly-hair routines**

## Implications for marketers

Segment the shelf: protect volume on basics with value packs, and build premium, hair-type-specific treatment lines with clear before/after evidence.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 35% of buyers.
- Build the offer around quality & durability, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 42% of purchases.
