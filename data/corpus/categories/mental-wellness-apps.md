# Mental Wellness Apps: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: meditation apps, therapy apps, mental health apps, journaling apps. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

Mental Wellness Apps reached 19% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €11 per month. The category is growing modestly (+6.8% spend vs the 2025 wave). Gen Z talks openly about mental health but pays reluctantly for apps; free trials convert poorly unless the app becomes a daily habit within a week.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 19%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €11
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €25
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +6.8%
- Buyers in panel: 1,139 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 24% of women bought versus 13% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 17% among 16-19 year-olds to 21% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in France (22%) and lowest in the UK (16%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€14 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 22%, compared with 17% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (30% of buyers), followed by Search engines (20%) and Friends & family (18%). TikTok accounts for 36% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 26% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (21% vs 17%).

The main purchase channel is Subscription (88%), then Brand website / app (12%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of convenience (29%) and brand identity & values (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 60% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are subscription price and doubt about effectiveness.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

6.8% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (19 points below the panel average of 26%). 3.0% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (14 points below the panel average of 17%), and 27% usually repurchase the same brand (12 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among mental wellness apps buyers, the most common product type is Journaling app (40% of buyers). Journaling app over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (31% of its buyers), while Online therapy skews older (48% aged 25-29).

- **Journaling app**: 40% of category buyers, typical price €6/month, interest rising, mostly bought via Subscription.
- **Meditation app**: 39% of category buyers, typical price €10/month, interest stable, mostly bought via Subscription.
- **Online therapy**: 20% of category buyers, typical price €60/month, interest rising, mostly bought via Subscription.

## Trends to watch

- **Journaling apps**
- **Therapy-speak**
- **Digital detox**

## Implications for marketers

Optimise for week-one habit formation and consider employer/university partnerships to remove the price barrier.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 30% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Subscription, which carries 88% of purchases.
