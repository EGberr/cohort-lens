# Mental Wellness Apps: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: meditation apps, therapy apps, mental health apps, journaling apps. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

Mental Wellness Apps reached 19% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €12 per month. The category is growing modestly (+6.8% spend vs the 2025 wave). Gen Z talks openly about mental health but pays reluctantly for apps; free trials convert poorly unless the app becomes a daily habit within a week.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 19%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €12
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €27
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +6.8%
- Buyers in panel: 1,153 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 25% of women bought versus 13% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 16% among 16-19 year-olds to 22% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in France (24%) and lowest in the UK (14%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€15 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 24%, compared with 16% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (30% of buyers), followed by Friends & family (23%) and Search engines (19%). TikTok accounts for 33% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 24% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (20% vs 18%).

The main purchase channel is Subscription (89%), then Brand website / app (11%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of convenience (29%) and brand identity & values (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 61% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are subscription price and doubt about effectiveness.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

5.9% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (19 points below the panel average of 25%). 3.3% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (14 points below the panel average of 17%), and 28% usually repurchase the same brand (10 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Optimise for week-one habit formation and consider employer/university partnerships to remove the price barrier.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 30% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Subscription, which carries 89% of purchases.
