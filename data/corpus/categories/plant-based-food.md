# Plant-based Food: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: vegan food, plant-based meat, oat milk, meat alternatives. Sector: Food & Beverage.*

## Executive summary

Plant-based Food reached 42% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €28 per month. The category is in decline (-2.8% spend vs the 2025 wave). Plant-based is shifting from meat imitation to 'whole-food' plant protein: alt-meat stalls while tofu, legumes and oat milk keep growing.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 42%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €28
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €141
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: -2.8%
- Buyers in panel: 2,493 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 50% of women bought versus 33% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 35% among 16-19 year-olds to 49% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Italy (45%) and lowest in Spain (38%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€35 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 50%, compared with 36% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by In store (30% of buyers), followed by TikTok (21%) and Friends & family (20%). TikTok accounts for 22% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 19% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (7.2% vs 4.9%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (80%), then Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (7.7%) and Subscription (6.3%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (42% of buyers cite it first), ahead of sustainability (28%) and price / value for money (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 66% of buyers, 5 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are price premium vs meat and ultra-processed perception.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

55% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (30 points above the panel average of 25%). 11% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (6 points below the panel average of 17%), and 29% usually repurchase the same brand (9 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Whole-food protein**
- **Flexitarian**
- **Ultra-processed backlash**

## Implications for marketers

Pivot messaging from imitation to clean, whole-food credentials and close the price gap through private-label partnerships.

- Prioritise In store for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 30% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 80% of purchases.
