# Period Care: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: menstrual products, period underwear, menstrual cups, tampons. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

43% of Gen Z consumers bought period care in the last twelve months, spending an average of €9 per month. The category is growing modestly (+4.6% spend vs the 2025 wave). Reusables (period underwear, cups) are growing fastest among older Gen Z, while loyalty to a single brand of disposables remains high.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 43%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €9
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €48
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +4.6%
- Buyers in panel: 2,562 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 63% of women bought versus 20% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 40% among 16-19 year-olds to 44% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Italy (48%) and lowest in Spain (38%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€12 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 45%, compared with 42% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by In store (28% of buyers), followed by Friends & family (25%) and TikTok (23%). TikTok accounts for 23% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 23% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (7.7% vs 8.9%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (62%), then Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (17%) and Subscription (12%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of sustainability (30%) and quality & durability (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 56% of buyers, 5 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are price of reusables and comfort and leakage concerns.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

53% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (28 points above the panel average of 25%). 3.9% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (13 points below the panel average of 17%), and 62% usually repurchase the same brand (23 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Lower the entry price of reusables with starter kits and use open, educational content to build trust.

- Prioritise In store for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 28% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 62% of purchases.
