# Period Care: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: menstrual products, period underwear, menstrual cups, tampons. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

43% of Gen Z consumers bought period care in the last twelve months, spending an average of €9 per month. The category is growing modestly (+4.6% spend vs the 2025 wave). Reusables (period underwear, cups) are growing fastest among older Gen Z, while loyalty to a single brand of disposables remains high.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 43%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €9
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €48
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +4.6%
- Buyers in panel: 2,572 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 62% of women bought versus 22% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 40% among 16-19 year-olds to 44% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in the UK (52%) and lowest in France (36%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€11 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 49%, compared with 40% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by In store (27% of buyers), followed by Friends & family (26%) and TikTok (23%). TikTok accounts for 27% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 22% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (9.9% vs 7.0%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (60%), then Online marketplaces (16%) and Subscription (13%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of sustainability (29%) and quality & durability (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 54% of buyers, 8 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are price of reusables and comfort and leakage concerns.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

53% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (27 points above the panel average of 26%). 4.5% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (12 points below the panel average of 17%), and 63% usually repurchase the same brand (25 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among period care buyers, the most common product type is Organic tampons (45% of buyers). Organic tampons over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (32% of its buyers), while Period underwear skews older (42% aged 25-29).

- **Organic tampons**: 45% of category buyers, typical price €5, interest stable, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Period underwear**: 33% of category buyers, typical price €30, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Menstrual cup**: 21% of category buyers, typical price €22, interest stable, mostly bought via Physical store.

## Implications for marketers

Lower the entry price of reusables with starter kits and use open, educational content to build trust.

- Prioritise In store for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 27% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 60% of purchases.
