# Investing Apps: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: stock trading apps, investing, ETFs, crypto apps. Sector: Money & Services.*

## Executive summary

Investing Apps reached 27% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €70 per month. The category is growing solidly (+10.3% spend vs the 2025 wave). FinTok drives first investments; Gen Z starts with ETFs and fractional shares, but trust in 'finfluencers' is falling.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 27%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €70
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €226
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +10.3%
- Buyers in panel: 1,612 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards men: 38% of men bought versus 16% of women. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 20% among 16-19 year-olds to 33% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in France (36%) and lowest in the US (24%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€84 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 38%, compared with 21% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by YouTube (31% of buyers), followed by TikTok (26%) and Friends & family (21%). TikTok accounts for 29% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 23% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (14% vs 15%).

The main purchase channel is Brand website / app (93%), then Subscription (6.9%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is performance & specs (42% of buyers cite it first), ahead of convenience (28%) and community & belonging (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 49% of buyers, 12 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are fear of losing money and lack of knowledge.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

15% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (10 points below the panel average of 25%). 1.3% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (16 points below the panel average of 17%), and 58% usually repurchase the same brand (20 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Educate rather than hype; transparent fees and long-term ETF plans build trust with first-time investors.

- Prioritise YouTube for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 31% of buyers.
- Build the offer around performance & specs, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Brand website / app, which carries 93% of purchases.
