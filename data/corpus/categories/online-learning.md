# Online Learning: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: language learning, online courses, Duolingo, skills courses. Sector: Money & Services.*

## Executive summary

33% of Gen Z consumers bought online learning in the last twelve months, spending an average of €15 per month. The category is growing modestly (+4.9% spend vs the 2025 wave). Gamified learning keeps Gen Z engaged, but paid conversion depends on career outcomes — certificates must be recognised by employers.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 33%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €15
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €58
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +4.9%
- Buyers in panel: 1,977 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 36% of women bought versus 30% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 27% among 16-19 year-olds to 39% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in France (34%) and lowest in the US (29%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€18 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 38%, compared with 30% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (27% of buyers), followed by YouTube (24%) and Search engines (21%). TikTok accounts for 29% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 25% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (23% vs 20%).

The main purchase channel is Subscription (74%), then Brand website / app (23%) and Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (3.4%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is convenience (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (30%) and community & belonging (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 62% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are lack of motivation and doubts about certificate value.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

7.7% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (18 points below the panel average of 25%). 2.6% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (15 points below the panel average of 17%), and 36% usually repurchase the same brand (in line with the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Gamified learning**
- **Ai tutors**
- **Career micro-credentials**

## Implications for marketers

Link courses to concrete career outcomes and employer partnerships; streaks and community keep learners engaged.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 27% of buyers.
- Build the offer around convenience, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Subscription, which carries 74% of purchases.
