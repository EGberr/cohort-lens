# Low & No Alcohol: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: alcohol-free drinks, non-alcoholic beer, mocktails, sober curious. Sector: Food & Beverage.*

## Executive summary

34% of Gen Z consumers bought low & no alcohol in the last twelve months, spending an average of €22 per month. The category is one of the fastest-growing categories in the panel (+18.2% spend vs the 2025 wave). 'Sober curious' is mainstream: Gen Z drinks less and more intentionally, and non-alcoholic options need to feel like an adult social choice, not a compromise.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 34%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €22
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €90
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +18.2%
- Buyers in panel: 2,033 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 37% of women bought versus 31% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 28% among 16-19 year-olds to 40% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in the US (40%) and lowest in the UK (28%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€26 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 40%, compared with 30% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by Friends & family (26% of buyers), followed by TikTok (23%) and In store (20%). TikTok accounts for 27% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 21% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (6.4% vs 4.9%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (70%), then Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (13%) and Brand website / app (9.7%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of community & belonging (28%) and novelty & trends (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 52% of buyers, 9 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are price same as alcoholic drinks and taste.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

29% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (3 points above the panel average of 25%). 5.5% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (12 points below the panel average of 17%), and 37% usually repurchase the same brand (in line with the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Position NA drinks as premium social rituals (bars, events) and avoid 'healthy' framing that kills the fun.

- Prioritise Friends & family for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 26% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 70% of purchases.
