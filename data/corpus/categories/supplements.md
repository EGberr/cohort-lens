# Supplements: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: vitamins, protein powder, creatine, gummies. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

Supplements reached 41% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €26 per month. The category is growing solidly (+12.1% spend vs the 2025 wave). Creatine and magnesium are the breakout supplements; Gen Z wants third-party testing and is quick to call out exaggerated claims.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 41%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €26
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €125
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +12.1%
- Buyers in panel: 2,434 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

Purchase is balanced by gender (women 40%, men 41%). It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 37% among 16-19 year-olds to 44% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Italy (47%) and lowest in Spain (34%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€32 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 47%, compared with 37% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (34% of buyers), followed by YouTube (20%) and Instagram (16%). TikTok accounts for 38% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 31% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (14% vs 13%).

The main purchase channel is Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (38%), then Physical store (26%) and Brand website / app (15%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 12% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 9.2% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (43% of buyers cite it first), ahead of performance & specs (27%) and creator recommendation (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 56% of buyers, 5 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are distrust of claims and price.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

20% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (5 points below the panel average of 25%). 19% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (in line with the panel average of 17%), and 44% usually repurchase the same brand (6 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Creatine for everyone**
- **Magnesium for sleep**
- **Third-party testing**

## Implications for marketers

Publish third-party test results and dosage science; avoid miracle claims that invite creator backlash.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 34% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...), which carries 38% of purchases.
