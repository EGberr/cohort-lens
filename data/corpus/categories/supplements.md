# Supplements: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: vitamins, protein powder, creatine, gummies. Sector: Health & Wellness.*

## Executive summary

Supplements reached 42% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €25 per month. The category is growing solidly (+12.1% spend vs the 2025 wave). Creatine and magnesium are the breakout supplements; Gen Z wants third-party testing and is quick to call out exaggerated claims.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 42%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €25
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €127
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +12.1%
- Buyers in panel: 2,517 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

Purchase is balanced by gender (women 41%, men 43%). It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 40% among 16-19 year-olds to 44% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Spain (45%) and lowest in Italy (36%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€32 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 49%, compared with 38% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (34% of buyers), followed by YouTube (19%) and Instagram (17%). TikTok accounts for 37% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 34% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (14% vs 12%).

The main purchase channel is Online marketplaces (37%), then Physical store (28%) and Brand website / app (15%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 12% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 12% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of performance & specs (29%) and creator recommendation (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 57% of buyers, 4 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are distrust of claims and price.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

20% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (6 points below the panel average of 26%). 18% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (in line with the panel average of 17%), and 42% usually repurchase the same brand (4 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among supplements buyers, the most common product type is Protein powder (36% of buyers). Protein powder over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (29% of its buyers), while Magnesium skews older (40% aged 25-29).

- **Protein powder**: 36% of category buyers, typical price €35, interest stable, mostly bought via Online marketplaces.
- **Creatine**: 34% of category buyers, typical price €25, interest rising, mostly bought via Online marketplaces.
- **Magnesium**: 30% of category buyers, typical price €15, interest rising, mostly bought via Online marketplaces.

## Trends to watch

- **Creatine for everyone**
- **Magnesium for sleep**
- **Third-party testing**

## Implications for marketers

Publish third-party test results and dosage science; avoid miracle claims that invite creator backlash.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 34% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Online marketplaces, which carries 37% of purchases.
