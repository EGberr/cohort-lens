# Better-for-you Snacks: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: healthy snacks, protein bars, snacks, high-protein snacks. Sector: Food & Beverage.*

## Executive summary

63% of Gen Z consumers bought better-for-you snacks in the last twelve months, spending an average of €20 per month. The category is growing solidly (+9.1% spend vs the 2025 wave). Protein is the master claim: 'high-protein' versions of everyday snacks out-perform 'low-calorie' ones, reflecting a shift from diet culture to fitness culture.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 63%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €20
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €148
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +9.1%
- Buyers in panel: 3,785 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 67% of women bought versus 60% of men. Penetration is similar across age bands (16-19: 61%, 25-29: 65%). By market, penetration is highest in the UK (68%) and lowest in Spain (59%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€23 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 68%, compared with 60% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by In store (30% of buyers), followed by TikTok (28%) and Friends & family (14%). TikTok accounts for 32% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 25% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (7.5% vs 4.7%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (70%), then Online marketplaces (12%) and Subscription (6.2%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 8.2% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 5.2% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (30%) and novelty & trends (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 69% of buyers, 8 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are taste trade-offs and price per gram.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

29% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (3 points above the panel average of 26%). 21% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (4 points above the panel average of 17%), and 33% usually repurchase the same brand (6 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Products Gen Z buys

Among better-for-you snacks buyers, the most common product type is Protein bar (41% of buyers). Flavoured rice cakes over-indexes with 16-19 year-olds (30% of its buyers), while High-protein yogurt skews older (39% aged 25-29).

- **Protein bar**: 41% of category buyers, typical price €2, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **High-protein yogurt**: 32% of category buyers, typical price €2, interest rising, mostly bought via Physical store.
- **Flavoured rice cakes**: 27% of category buyers, typical price €2, interest stable, mostly bought via Physical store.

## Trends to watch

## Implications for marketers

Lead with protein and fibre rather than calorie claims; make the taste proof visible through creator taste tests.

- Prioritise In store for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 30% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 70% of purchases.
