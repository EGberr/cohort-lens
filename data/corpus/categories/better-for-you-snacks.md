# Better-for-you Snacks: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: healthy snacks, protein bars, snacks, high-protein snacks. Sector: Food & Beverage.*

## Executive summary

63% of Gen Z consumers bought better-for-you snacks in the last twelve months, spending an average of €20 per month. The category is growing solidly (+9.1% spend vs the 2025 wave). Protein is the master claim: 'high-protein' versions of everyday snacks out-perform 'low-calorie' ones, reflecting a shift from diet culture to fitness culture.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 63%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €20
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €148
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +9.1%
- Buyers in panel: 3,783 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 68% of women bought versus 58% of men. Penetration is similar across age bands (16-19: 61%, 25-29: 65%). By market, penetration is highest in the US (67%) and lowest in the UK (60%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€25 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 66%, compared with 61% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (29% of buyers), followed by In store (29%) and Instagram (15%). TikTok accounts for 34% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 26% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (6.5% vs 6.4%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (69%), then Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (12%) and Brand website / app (6.7%).

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is health benefits (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (31%) and novelty & trends (19%). Price is the decisive factor for 70% of buyers, 9 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are taste trade-offs and price per gram.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

30% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (4 points above the panel average of 25%). 22% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (5 points above the panel average of 17%), and 32% usually repurchase the same brand (7 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Protein-maxxing**
- **Gut health**
- **Anti-diet culture**

## Implications for marketers

Lead with protein and fibre rather than calorie claims; make the taste proof visible through creator taste tests.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 29% of buyers.
- Build the offer around health benefits, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 69% of purchases.
