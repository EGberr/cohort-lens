# Bags & Accessories: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: handbags, backpacks, accessories, tote bags. Sector: Fashion & Accessories.*

## Executive summary

Over the past year, 44% of the Gen Z panel purchased bags & accessories, spending an average of €23 per month. The category is growing modestly (+2.6% spend vs the 2025 wave). Bags are the most common entry point to luxury for Gen Z — usually secondhand — while the mass market is dominated by designer-inspired dupes.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 44%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €23
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €122
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +2.6%
- Buyers in panel: 2,637 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 56% of women bought versus 31% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 41% among 16-19 year-olds to 47% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in the UK (48%) and lowest in the US (40%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€27 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 52%, compared with 40% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (33% of buyers), followed by Instagram (30%) and In store (13%). TikTok accounts for 37% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 29% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (8.4% vs 7.3%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (30%), then Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (24%) and Brand website / app (20%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 9.7% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 6.3% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is aesthetics & self-expression (41% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (29%) and quality & durability (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 64% of buyers, in line with the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are dupes of designer bags and durability.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

32% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (6 points above the panel average of 25%). 48% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (31 points above the panel average of 17%), and 26% usually repurchase the same brand (13 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **First-luxury purchase**
- **Certified pre-owned**
- **Bag charms**

## Implications for marketers

Offer a credible 'first luxury' tier and certified pre-owned programmes; mass brands should invest in original design signatures to escape dupe comparisons.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 33% of buyers.
- Build the offer around aesthetics & self-expression, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 30% of purchases.
