# Color Cosmetics: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: makeup, lipstick, foundation, mascara. Sector: Beauty & Personal Care.*

## Executive summary

Over the past year, 65% of the Gen Z panel purchased color cosmetics, spending an average of €30 per month. The category is growing modestly (+4.1% spend vs the 2025 wave). Makeup is the most 'dupe-native' category in the panel: a majority bought a lookalike of a prestige product, and the dupe is often discovered in the same video that reviews the original.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 65%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €30
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €232
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +4.1%
- Buyers in panel: 3,887 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 79% of women bought versus 48% of men. Penetration is similar across age bands (16-19: 66%, 25-29: 63%). By market, penetration is highest in France (71%) and lowest in Germany (61%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€36 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 70%, compared with 63% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (51% of buyers), followed by Instagram (18%) and YouTube (14%). TikTok accounts for 56% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 50% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (4.9% vs 3.8%).

The main purchase channel is Physical store (31%), then TikTok Shop (27%) and Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (16%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 30% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 27% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is creator recommendation (42% of buyers cite it first), ahead of price / value for money (30%) and aesthetics & self-expression (18%). Price is the decisive factor for 68% of buyers, 7 points above the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are shade-range gaps and fear of wasting money on a viral product.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

33% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (7 points above the panel average of 25%). 59% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (42 points above the panel average of 17%), and 26% usually repurchase the same brand (13 points below the panel average of 39%).

## Trends to watch

- **Dupe culture**
- **No-makeup makeup**
- **Blush revival**

## Implications for marketers

Treat dupes as a competitive reality: defend prestige lines with performance claims and refill/value sizes, and launch entry price points instead of fighting comparison content.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 51% of buyers.
- Build the offer around creator recommendation, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Physical store, which carries 31% of purchases.
