# Athleisure & Activewear: Gen Z Category Report 2026

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

*Also covers: activewear, leggings, gym clothes, sportswear. Sector: Fashion & Accessories.*

## Executive summary

Athleisure & Activewear reached 57% of Gen Z consumers over the last twelve months, spending an average of €35 per month. The category is growing solidly (+8.8% spend vs the 2025 wave). Activewear is the uniform of the 'that girl' and gym-tok aesthetic; community (run clubs, studio collectives) converts better than pure product ads.

## Market snapshot

- Penetration (bought in last 12 months): 57%
- Average monthly spend among buyers: €35
- Annualised spend per Gen Z consumer: €237
- Spend growth vs 2025 wave: +8.8%
- Buyers in panel: 3,424 of 6,000 respondents

## Who buys

The category skews towards women: 66% of women bought versus 48% of men. It is an older-Gen Z category: penetration rises from 53% among 16-19 year-olds to 60% among 25-29 year-olds. By market, penetration is highest in Spain (63%) and lowest in France (47%). Buyers in the US spend the most (€43 per month). Among high-income respondents penetration reaches 64%, compared with 52% in the low-income band.

## Path to purchase

Discovery is led by TikTok (35% of buyers), followed by Instagram (28%) and YouTube (12%). TikTok accounts for 39% of first discoveries among 16-19 year-olds versus 32% among 25-29 year-olds, while search engines matter more for the older group (7.3% vs 4.9%).

The main purchase channel is Brand website / app (32%), then Physical store (26%) and Marketplaces (Amazon, Zalando...) (20%). Social commerce through TikTok Shop reaches 17% of purchases among the youngest buyers, against 12% among 25-29 year-olds.

## Purchase drivers and barriers

The top purchase driver is quality & durability (40% of buyers cite it first), ahead of aesthetics & self-expression (29%) and community & belonging (20%). Price is the decisive factor for 57% of buyers, 4 points below the panel average of 61%. The main barriers reported in open answers are premium prices and sizing inconsistency.

## Values: sustainability, dupes and loyalty

36% of buyers say they would pay more for a sustainable option (10 points above the panel average of 25%). 38% bought a dupe or lookalike product in the last year (20 points above the panel average of 17%), and 42% usually repurchase the same brand (4 points above the panel average of 39%).

## Implications for marketers

Sponsor communities, not just athletes: run clubs and studio partnerships drive both discovery and repeat purchase.

- Prioritise TikTok for awareness: it is the first discovery source for 35% of buyers.
- Build the offer around quality & durability, the most-cited driver.
- Plan conversion on Brand website / app, which carries 32% of purchases.
