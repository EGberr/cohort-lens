# The Sustainability Say-Do Gap

> **Synthetic data notice.** This report is part of a fictional demo corpus generated for Cohort Lens. Figures come from a simulated panel and must not be used for real decisions.

## Summary

On average 26% of buyers say they would pay a premium for a sustainable option, but willingness varies strongly by category. Fast fashion illustrates the gap: it still reaches 68% of Gen Z although only 30% of its buyers would pay more for sustainability.

## Highest willingness to pay for sustainability

- Secondhand & Resale Fashion: 66%
- Plant-based Food: 56%
- Electric Vehicles: 55%
- Period Care: 53%
- Skincare: 48%
- Micromobility: 42%
- Demi-fine Jewelry: 41%
- Specialty Coffee: 39%
- Houseplants: 39%
- Pet Care: 38%

## Lowest willingness to pay

- Mobile Gaming (In-app Purchases): 3.9%
- Buy Now, Pay Later: 4.7%
- Video Streaming: 5.2%
- Music Streaming: 6.1%
- Mental Wellness Apps: 6.8%

## Implications

Sustainability converts when it is tangible and personal — resale, reusables, refills — and rarely when it is an abstract claim. Circular models (trade-in, resale credit) let Gen Z act on values without paying more.
